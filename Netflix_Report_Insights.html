Objective

Understand what Netflix’s catalog is made of (type, origin, genre, audience and timing) and turn it into a 3-page interactive dashboard: a landing page, an overview, and a geography & genres page.

Dashboard structure

1 · Landing

Top 10 countries as rotating flags, 3 flip KPIs, Netflix branding.

2 · Overview

5 KPIs and 4 charts: movies vs TV, additions by year, audience rating, additions by month.

3 · Geography & Genres

Top countries with flags, top genres, release decades, and a smooth additions-per-year line.

Built with

Python and Power Query for cleaning · Power BI data model (Netflix_Clean, Netflix_Genres, Flags) · DAX measures generating HTML/CSS visuals · dark and light themes.

Limitations to keep in mind

  • Each title has one country only, so geography shows the main production country.
  • 29% of titles have no director listed.
  • 2021 is a partial year: the data stops on 25 September 2021.
  • 14 titles show an added date earlier than their release year; they are flagged, not removed.

Aha moment

Netflix’s catalog is young, adult-focused and concentrated.

Half of it comes from two countries, most of it is recent, and additions peaked in 2019 (2,013) before cooling to 1,879 in 2020 and 1,498 in 2021 (a partial year). The catalog is built for volume and freshness more than depth.

4

Solution & next steps

Grow regional hubs

Egypt has 123 titles (11th overall), South Korea 214 and Spain 182: strong room to scale local content.

Close the family gap

Only 10% of titles target kids. A clear whitespace for family and young-audience content.

Invest in depth

With two-thirds of shows at one season, renewals and longer arcs could lift retention.

Track the full 2021

Re-run the dashboard on a full-year dataset to confirm whether the slowdown is real.
